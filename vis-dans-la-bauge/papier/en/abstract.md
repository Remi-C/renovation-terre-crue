# Screws in cob — abstract

Rémi Cura, PhD — Clos Poulain, near Rennes — November 2026

**The question.** Can you screw into cob (French *bauge*)? Earth building guides say yes, for light loads, but give
no detail: no diameter, no length, no load. We tested an old, dry cob wall, with ordinary tools, to give builders those
figures.

**What we found.** With good-quality fully threaded structural timber screws, 8 and 10 mm in diameter, 300 kg sideways
(in shear) is easily reached: 200 kg from 5.5 cm of screw in the cob with Ø10, at least 300 kg from 9.5 cm. These
figures hold for a screw fixing a timber batten against the wall, stiffer than a screw alone. In pull-out (screw pulled
out of the wall), a screw holds less: a Ø10 gave way at 70 kg 7 cm in, at 130 kg at 9 cm, at 220 kg at 11.5 cm, and
held 300 to 400 kg from 12.5 to 17.5 cm. Up to 11.5 cm at least, pull-out is what limits.

**Driving and advice.** A screw always works both sideways and in pull-out, and we do not know how the two interact:
the author takes the weaker of the two strengths. The paper gives no safe load, which depends on too many things (the
cob of the wall, how the screw is loaded, knocks, what happens if it gives way); it sets the measurements next to what
published texts advise: screws of 10 to 20 cm and, for other earth walls, 3 to 50 kg per fixing, no text giving a load
for cob. For more, use more screws, or embed a timber in the cob; and always plan for redundancy, as cob is not
uniform. For the Ø10, a 5 to 6 mm pilot hole clearly reduces the driving torque and keeps
within the manufacturer's recommendations.
