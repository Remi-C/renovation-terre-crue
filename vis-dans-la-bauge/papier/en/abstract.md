# Screws in cob — abstract

Rémi Cura, PhD — Clos Poulain, near Rennes — November 2026

**The question.** Can you screw into cob (French *bauge*)? Earth building guides say yes, for light loads, but give
no detail: no diameter, no length, no load. We tested an old, dry cob wall, with ordinary tools, to give builders those
figures.

**What we found.** With good-quality fully threaded structural timber screws, 8 and 10 mm in diameter, 300 kg sideways
(in shear) is easily reached. What matters most is the length of screw in the cob: 200 kg from 5.5 cm with Ø10 and from
7.5 cm with Ø8; at least 300 kg from 9.5 cm with Ø10; a small Ø6 screw 3.5 cm in already holds 40 kg. These figures
hold for a screw fixing a timber batten against the wall, stiffer than a screw alone.

**Driving and advice.** For the Ø10, a 5 to 6 mm pilot hole clearly reduces the driving torque and keeps within the
manufacturer's recommendations; which pilot hole for which diameter and which depth remains to be measured. Even if a
screw holds 300 kg, plan for redundancy: a local flaw in the cob must not compromise the structure. There is no
official margin for cob: the paper gives, by analogy, those used for earth and other materials. Pull-out (screw pulled
along its axis) is the subject of a second campaign, in November 2026.
